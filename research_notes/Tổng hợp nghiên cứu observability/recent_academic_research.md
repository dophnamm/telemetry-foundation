# Recent Academic Research (2021–2026) on Observability for Cloud-Native and Microservice Systems

Scope note for the report writer: every bibliographic record below was checked against arXiv API metadata, Crossref DOI records, or the publisher/conference page (October 2026). "Preprint" means arXiv-only (not peer-reviewed) at the time of checking. ★ marks the ~25 core picks for a practitioner-oriented annotated list; the rest are supporting or "watch list" items. Each entry follows this order: **Title**. Authors. Year. Venue. Link. *Finding*. **Why it matters for OTel + Prometheus/Loki/Tempo/Grafana**.

## Q1. Which surveys and systematic reviews (2021–2026) cover observability, distributed tracing, and microservice monitoring?

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed systematic review is devoted only to OpenTelemetry. The best starting points are four peer-reviewed surveys: Soldani & Brogi (ACM CSUR 2022) on anomaly detection and RCA, Usman et al. (IEEE Access 2022) on observability, Li et al. (EMSE) on tracing practice in industry, and Zhang et al. (ACM CSUR 2025) on LLM-era AIOps. Two newer arXiv RCA surveys (2024, 2025) add more recent coverage.

### Cited Findings
- ★ **Anomaly Detection and Failure Root Cause Analysis in (Micro)Service-Based Cloud Applications: A Survey**. Jacopo Soldani, Antonio Brogi. 2022. *ACM Computing Surveys*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3501297 (preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12378). *A structured review of anomaly-detection and RCA techniques for multi-service apps, organized by the telemetry each one consumes (logs, traces, metrics/KPIs).* **Why it matters:** it is the standard taxonomy for linking each signal type (Prometheus metrics, Tempo traces, Loki logs) to the class of detection or RCA method that can use it. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3501297); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12378)
- ★ **A Survey on Observability of Distributed Edge & Container-Based Microservices**. Muhammad Usman, Simone Ferlin, Anna Brunstrom, Javid Taheri. 2022. *IEEE Access*. https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3193102. *A survey of observability (logging, metrics, tracing, and the tooling for them) for container-based and edge microservices.* **Why it matters:** a general entry point to observability concepts and tooling for Kubernetes and edge deployments. — [DOI via Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3193102)
- ★ **Enjoy your observability: an industrial survey of microservice tracing and analysis**. Bowen Li, Xin Peng, Qilin Xiang, Hanzhang Wang, Tao Xie, Jun Sun, Xuanzhe Liu. Published online 2021, *Empirical Software Engineering* vol. 27, issue 1 (2022). https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-021-10063-9. *Interviews with developers and operations engineers at 10 companies. Large systems run a tracing-and-analysis pipeline with different trade-offs. Visualization and statistical metrics are the most common analysis methods; ML and data mining are seldom used.* **Why it matters:** this is real-world evidence that the work most teams actually do on traces is Grafana/Tempo-style visualization plus RED-style metrics derived from spans, not advanced AIOps. — [SMU InK record](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sis_research/6976); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-021-10063-9)
- **Automated Analysis of Distributed Tracing: Challenges and Research Directions**. Andre Bento, Jaime Correia, Ricardo Filipe, Filipe Araujo, Jorge Cardoso. 2021. *Journal of Grid Computing*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10723-021-09551-5. *A review of the challenges in automatically analyzing tracing data, with research directions.* **Why it matters:** it frames why raw Tempo/Jaeger traces are hard to use at scale without automated analysis. — [DOI via Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10723-021-09551-5)
- **A Survey of AIOps Methods for Failure Management**. Paolo Notaro, Jorge Cardoso, Michael Gerndt. 2021. *ACM Transactions on Intelligent Systems and Technology*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3483424. *A pre-LLM taxonomy of AIOps methods for failure management (detection, prediction, root-cause analysis, remediation).* **Why it matters:** the baseline vocabulary of the field before LLMs. — [DOI via Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1145/3483424)
- ★ **A Survey of AIOps in the Era of Large Language Models**. Lingzhe Zhang, Tong Jia, Mengxi Jia, Yifan Wu, Aiwei Liu, Yong Yang, Zhonghai Wu, Xuming Hu, Philip S. Yu, Ying Li. 2025. *ACM Computing Surveys*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3746635 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12472). It extends the 2024 arXiv survey "A Survey of AIOps for Failure Management in the Era of Large Language Models" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11213). *Analyzes 183 papers (Jan 2020–Dec 2024) on LLMs for AIOps, covering data sources, task evolution, methods, and evaluation.* **Why it matters:** the most complete map of LLM-based incident tooling, for anyone weighing "AI assistants" built on top of Grafana data. — [arXiv 2507.12472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12472); [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1145/3746635)
- ★ **A Goal-Driven Survey on Root Cause Analysis**. Aoyang Fang, Haowen Yang, Haoze Dong, Qisheng Lu, Junjielong Xu, Pinjia He. 2025. *arXiv preprint*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19593. *Groups 135 RCA papers (2014–2025) by goal (for example, fast triage that localizes a faulty service vs. a definitive bug fix) rather than by input data type. The authors argue that grouping by data type hides real progress.* **Why it matters:** it helps practitioners match a tool's RCA claim to their actual need (paging triage vs. postmortem fix). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19593)
- **A Comprehensive Survey on Root Cause Analysis in (Micro) Services: Methodologies, Challenges, and Trends**. Tingting Wang, Guilin Qi. 2024. *arXiv preprint*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00803. *Reviews RCA techniques that use metrics, traces, logs, and multi-modal data.* **Why it matters:** an organized method catalog for each telemetry type. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00803)
- **A Survey of Time Series Anomaly Detection Methods in the AIOps Domain**. Zhenyu Zhong, Qiliang Fan, Jiacheng Zhang, Minghua Ma, Shenglin Zhang, Yongqian Sun, Qingwei Lin, Yuzhi Zhang, Dan Pei. 2023. *arXiv preprint*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00393. *An overview of univariate and multivariate KPI anomaly detection in AIOps.* **Why it matters:** this is the research behind "smarter than static threshold" alerting on Prometheus metrics. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00393)

### Inferences
- The survey literature is RCA- and AIOps-heavy. Observability engineering itself (instrumentation design, pipelines, cost) is covered mainly by Usman et al. 2022 and the Li et al. industrial study. That suggests a research gap that practitioner sources currently fill.
- Li et al. found that ML-based trace analysis was rare in industry. If that still holds, most of the RCA research in Q2/Q3 remains ahead of mainstream practice.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed systematic literature review or mapping study devoted specifically to distributed tracing or to OpenTelemetry for 2023–2026. An arXiv title search for "distributed tracing" + survey/review/mapping returned nothing relevant.
- I did not retrieve the full text of the Usman et al. or Bento et al. surveys, so their summaries above are kept general.

## Q2. What is the state of RCA and anomaly detection over traces, metrics, and logs (AIOps), including benchmarks and datasets?

### Takeaway
The field has moved from single-signal methods to multi-modal methods that fuse traces, metrics, and logs (Eadro, Nezha), and toward shared benchmarks (RCAEval, PetShop, LO2, OpenRCA). Rigorous evaluations keep finding that no method wins everywhere, and 2026 audits warn that pooled leaderboards mislead.

### Cited Findings
- ★ **Root Cause Analysis for Microservice System based on Causal Inference: How Far Are We?**. Luan Pham, Huong Ha, Hongyu Zhang. 2024. *ASE 2024*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3691620.3695065 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13729). *Evaluates 9 causal-discovery and 21 RCA methods; "no method stands out in all situations."* **Why it matters:** be skeptical of any single "causal AI" RCA product claim. Results depend on the system and the fault type. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13729); [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1145/3691620.3695065)
- ★ **RCAEval: A Benchmark for Root Cause Analysis of Microservice Systems with Telemetry Data**. Luan Pham, Hongyu Zhang, Huong Ha, Flora Salim, Xiuzhen Zhang. 2025. *Companion Proceedings of the ACM Web Conference (WWW) 2025*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3701716.3715290 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17015). *Open benchmark with 735 failure cases from 3 microservice systems and 15 reproducible baselines, supporting both coarse- and fine-grained RCA.* **Why it matters:** a ready test harness to compare RCA approaches before adopting one on your own Prometheus/Tempo data. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17015); [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1145/3701716.3715290)
- ★ **Nezha: Interpretable Fine-Grained Root Causes Analysis for Microservices on Multi-modal Observability Data**. Guangba Yu, Pengfei Chen, Yufeng Li, Hongyang Chen, Xiaoyun Li, Zibin Zheng. 2023. *ESEC/FSE 2023*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3611643.3616249 (code: https://github.com/IntelligentDDS/Nezha). *Converts metrics, traces, and logs into one event representation, mines event graphs, and compares fault-free with faulty patterns. It reaches 89.77% average top-1 accuracy at code-region and resource-type level on two benchmarks.* **Why it matters:** it shows the payoff of correlating all three OTel signals (shared trace/span IDs across metrics exemplars, logs, and traces), not just storing them. — [FSE 2023 program](https://2023.esec-fse.org/details/fse-2023-research-papers/8/-Remote-Nezha-Interpretable-Fine-Grained-Root-Causes-Analysis-for-Microservices-on-); [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1145/3611643.3616249)
- ★ **Eadro: An End-to-End Troubleshooting Framework for Microservices on Multi-source Data**. Cheryl Lee, Tianyi Yang, Zhuangbin Chen, Yuxin Su, Michael R. Lyu. 2023. *ICSE 2023*. https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE48619.2023.00150 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05092). *Argues that trace-only methods miss anomalies, and jointly learns anomaly detection and root-cause localization from traces, logs, and KPIs.* **Why it matters:** independent support for collecting logs and metrics alongside traces rather than relying on Tempo alone. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05092); [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE48619.2023.00150)
- ★ **LO2: Microservice API Anomaly Dataset of Logs and Metrics**. Alexander Bakhtin, Jesse Nyyssölä, Yuqing Wang, Noman Ahmad, Ke Ping, Matteo Esposito, Mika Mäntylä, Davide Taibi. 2025. *PROMISE 2025*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3727582.3728682 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12067). *A multi-modal dataset (logs, metrics, traces) from dynamic API testing of a production microservice system. A follow-up, "Descriptor: An Improved Microservice Dataset of Logs and Metrics (LO2v2)", appeared in IEEE Data Descriptions in 2026 (DOI 10.1109/IEEEDATA.2026.3701668).* **Why it matters:** an open dataset for testing log and metric anomaly-detection pipelines similar to a Loki + Prometheus setup. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12067); [Crossref LO2](https://doi.org/10.1145/3727582.3728682); [Crossref LO2v2](https://doi.org/10.1109/ieeedata.2026.3701668)
- **The PetShop Dataset — Finding Causes of Performance Issues across Microservices**. Michaela Hardt, William R. Orchard, Patrick Blöbaum, Shiva Kasiviswanathan, Elke Kirschbaum (Amazon). 2024. *CLeaR 2024 (PMLR vol. 236)*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04806 (repo: https://github.com/amazon-science/petshop-root-cause-analysis). *A dataset for causal RCA of performance issues across microservices.* **Why it matters:** a public RCA dataset from industry. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04806)
- **Pooled Leaderboards Hide System-Specific Winners: A Reporting-Protocol Audit of Offline Root-Cause Analysis Benchmarks**. Lining Hu, Ting Liu, Yuzhuo Fu. 2026. *arXiv preprint*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29159. *Audits the OpenRCA, RCAEval, and PetShop benchmarks (11 subsystems, 778 matched units). When the method is chosen leave-one-system-out, the lower-scoring method is picked on up to 5 of 11 held-out subsystems, with regret up to 24.8 percentage points on RCAEval/Sock-Shop.* **Why it matters:** a top leaderboard rank does not predict performance on your system, so evaluate on your own telemetry. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29159)
- Older testbeds such as TrainTicket remain the standard fault-injection targets. For example, Cloud-OpsBench (2026) evaluates on OnlineBoutique and TrainTicket. — [arXiv 2603.00468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00468)

### Inferences
- Multi-modal correlation is where the gains are (Nezha, Eadro). For practitioners, this argues for consistent OTel resource attributes and trace-ID propagation into logs (Loki derived fields) and metrics (exemplars), so that signals can be joined.
- Benchmark results depend on the system. The 2024 "How Far Are We?" study and the 2026 audit independently agree that the right RCA method varies with the system and fault type.

### Gaps
- I did not separately verify the original publications of TrainTicket and DeathStarBench, which predate 2021. They are used as testbeds by the papers above.
- DiagFusion (IEEE TSC 2023) and MULAN (WWW 2024) were not found on arXiv or Crossref under their titles, so they are omitted rather than cited unverified.

## Q3. What research exists on LLMs and agents for incident management, RCA, and querying observability data?

### Takeaway
Industrial work from Microsoft and Alibaba (2023–2024) showed that LLMs help with incident RCA. Agent benchmarks from 2025–2026 (OpenRCA, AIOpsLab, ITBench, Cloud-OpsBench, ORCA-bench) consistently find low success rates (roughly 10–25% on realistic tasks). They also find that agents can name the right service without evidence-grounded reasoning. Natural-language-to-query work now targets PromQL and LogQL directly.

### Inferences
- Agent benchmarks are converging on realistic stacks (OTel Demo; Prometheus/Jaeger/Grafana in ORCA-bench). Practitioners can reuse these setups to evaluate vendor or in-house SRE agents.
- Success rates have stayed low over two years (11.34% in OpenRCA, 13.8% of SRE scenarios in ITBench, 10–25% in ORCA-bench). Evidence-grounding gaps (Cloud-OpsBench, Lu et al.) support using LLMs as query generators and summarizers with a human approving actions, not as autonomous root-causers.
- Text-to-PromQL (69.1%) and text-to-LogQL (fine-tuning helps substantially) suggest that schema and metric-catalog context is the main lever. Good metric naming and OTel semantic conventions likely improve LLM query accuracy. This is an inference; the papers did not test OTel naming directly.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed paper on LLM generation of TraceQL (Tempo's query language).
- I could not confirm whether ITBench also appeared at ICML 2025. Only the arXiv and DSN-S 2025 versions are verified.
- The 2026 preprints (ORCA-bench, Cloud-OpsBench, Lu et al., Cai et al., Tao et al.) are not yet peer-reviewed.

## Q4. What research addresses trace sampling, telemetry cost and overhead reduction, and telemetry compression?

### Takeaway
Research is moving past the head-vs-tail sampling dilemma. Hindsight (NSDI 2023) samples retroactively. Mint (ASPLOS 2025) and Tracezip (ISSTA 2025) keep every request in compressed or aggregated form. AutoScope (TOSEM 2026) samples at span level. Adaptive and learned samplers (TraStrainer, TraceMesh, RADAR) bias retention toward informative traces.

### Cited Findings
- ★ **The Benefit of Hindsight: Tracing Edge-Cases in Distributed Systems**. Lei Zhang, Vaastav Anand, Zhiqiang Xie, Ymir Vigfusson, Jonathan Mace. 2023. *USENIX NSDI 2023*. https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi23/presentation/zhang-lei (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05769). ### Inferences
- A strong research thread argues that "sample less" should become "store smarter." Mint's 2.7% storage figure and AutoScope's 81.2% reduction suggest large possible savings over probabilistic head sampling. However, these are research prototypes, not OTel Collector processors.
- Several of these papers come from the same research groups (Pengfei Chen and Zibin Zheng at Sun Yat-sen University; Michael R. Lyu at CUHK), so independent replication is limited.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed head-to-head evaluation of the OpenTelemetry Collector's tail_sampling processor against these research samplers.
- No metrics (Prometheus TSDB) cardinality or cost-reduction research was located within this search budget.

## Q5. What research covers eBPF-based / zero-code observability, and empirical studies of OpenTelemetry overhead or adoption?

### Takeaway
eBPF zero-code tracing is now peer-reviewed at top venues (DeepFlow, SIGCOMM 2023), with arXiv follow-ups on span correlation (Nahida, CrossTrace). The few rigorous OpenTelemetry-specific overhead studies show that overhead can be large (19–80% throughput loss in one ICPE 2025 study) and that exporting/serialization dominates it. I found no peer-reviewed empirical study of OpenTelemetry adoption.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The two peer-reviewed overhead studies differ greatly in setup (MooBench micro-benchmark on a Raspberry Pi vs. web-framework and serverless workloads). Overhead numbers should not be generalized across them, but both point to serialization/export as the cost center. That implies practical levers: batching, OTLP compression, sampling in the SDK, and a local Collector agent.
- Combining eBPF zero-code tracing (DeepFlow, Nahida) with SDK instrumentation looks complementary rather than competing. eBPF covers infrastructure and unmodified services but has span-correlation accuracy limits (Nahida tracks 92% of requests; CrossTrace correlates spans with over 90% accuracy).

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed empirical study of OpenTelemetry adoption or usage, for example mining GitHub repositories for OTel SDK usage or semantic-convention compliance. This is a clear gap in the literature.
- I found no peer-reviewed study measuring the overhead of OTel eBPF auto-instrumentation (OBI/Beyla) or of the OTel Collector itself.
- DeepFlow's quantitative claims (71 anomalies, 26 companies) come from search snippets of the paper's abstract. The full text was not read directly.
