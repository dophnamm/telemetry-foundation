# Self-hosted observability backends for a commercial on-prem / air-gapped product: license obligations, resource footprint, packaging

Scope: backends for metrics, logs, traces (plus dashboards/profiling) that a security software vendor would ship inside its own commercial on-prem product, including fully air-gapped sites, on Kubernetes with 20–100 services. Research date: 2026-10-07. **Not legal advice.** In each section, findings that quote license text or vendor statements are kept apart from interpretations (under "Inferences"). Every point that rests on interpretation should go to legal review before it decides anything.

---

## Q1. What does AGPLv3 require when a vendor ships Grafana, Loki, Tempo, Mimir, Pyroscope (and Alloy) inside a commercial on-prem product, unmodified vs modified vs embedded/linked? What does Grafana Labs say? Is there a commercial/OEM license?

### Takeaway
Shipping AGPL software to customers on-prem counts as "conveying". So even **unmodified** Grafana, Loki, Tempo, Mimir and Pyroscope bring GPLv3-style distribution duties: ship the license text and notices, provide the Corresponding Source (on the install media or through a 3-year written offer), and add no restrictions on those components in the EULA. The extra AGPL §13 network-source duty applies **only to modified versions**. The license text and the FSF FAQ treat the vendor's own code as outside the AGPL when it is a separate program in an "aggregate" that talks over sockets or HTTP. No court has tested that boundary; it is the FSF's interpretation. Grafana Labs says publicly that "unmodified distributions are not affected." But its free "Enterprise" binary **cannot** be redistributed: the EULA forbids distribution to third parties. The only non-AGPL route for bundling is a negotiated commercial/OEM license (oem@grafana.com). Alloy and Beyla are Apache-2.0.

### Cited Findings

**License text (AGPLv3, gnu.org)**
- §13 (the network clause) is triggered only by modification. Quote: "Notwithstanding any other provision of this License, **if you modify the Program**, your modified version must prominently offer all users interacting with it remotely through a computer network (if your version supports such interaction) an opportunity to receive the Corresponding Source of your version by providing access to the Corresponding Source from a network server at no charge…" — [AGPLv3 text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.txt)
- §4 (verbatim copies): you may convey verbatim copies if you "conspicuously and appropriately publish on each copy an appropriate copyright notice; keep intact all notices stating that this License … apply to the code; keep intact all notices of the absence of any warranty; and give all recipients a copy of this License along with the Program." — [AGPLv3 text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.txt)
- §6 (object code): "You may convey a covered work in object code form under the terms of sections 4 and 5, provided that you also convey the machine-readable Corresponding Source … in one of these ways." The ways are: (a) Corresponding Source "fixed on a durable physical medium" shipped with the product; (b) "a written offer, valid for at least three years and valid for as long as you offer spare parts or customer support for that product model"; (d) "offering access from a designated place … and offer equivalent access to the Corresponding Source in the same way through the same place". Under (d), "the Corresponding Source may be on a different server (operated by you or a third party) … provided you maintain clear directions next to the object code saying where to find" it. — [AGPLv3 text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.txt)
- Definition of "Corresponding Source": "all the source code needed to generate, install, and (for an executable work) run the object code and to modify the work, **including scripts to control those activities**." — [AGPLv3 text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.txt)
- §5 (aggregate): "A compilation of a covered work with other separate and independent works, which are not by their nature extensions of the covered work, and which are not combined with it such as to form a larger program, in or on a volume of a storage or distribution medium, is called an 'aggregate' … Inclusion of a covered work in an aggregate does not cause this License to apply to the other parts of the aggregate." — [AGPLv3 text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.txt)
- §10: "You may not impose any further restrictions on the exercise of the rights granted or affirmed under this License." — [AGPLv3 text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.txt)
- The "Installation Information" (anti-tivoization) duty applies only to "User Products", defined as "(1) a 'consumer product' … normally used for personal, family, or household purposes, or (2) anything designed or sold for incorporation into a dwelling." — [AGPLv3 text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.txt)

**FSF interpretation (GPL FAQ)**
- Aggregate and separate programs: "pipes, sockets and command-line arguments are communication mechanisms normally used between two separate programs. So when they are used for communication, the modules normally are separate programs." — [GNU GPL FAQ #MereAggregation](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#MereAggregation)
- "You cannot incorporate GPL-covered software in a proprietary system." The FAQ adds that you may distribute it alongside a proprietary system if the two stay well separated. — [GNU GPL FAQ #GPLInProprietarySystem](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#GPLInProprietarySystem)
- "The GNU Affero GPL requires that modified versions of the software offer all users interacting with it over a computer network an opportunity to receive the source." — [GNU GPL FAQ #UnreleasedModsAGPL](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#UnreleasedModsAGPL)

### Inferences
(Interpretation. Needs legal review.)
- **Unmodified AGPL components, each in its own container/process, shipped on-prem.** Obligations: (1) ship the AGPL license text and keep notices; (2) provide the Corresponding Source for the *exact* shipped versions. Because the build/install scripts in the definition arguably cover Dockerfiles and build scripts for the images you ship, the safest choice for fully air-gapped sites is §6(a): put source tarballs plus build scripts on the install media. §6(b), a written offer valid at least 3 years and as long as you support the product, is the alternative. Linking to upstream GitHub under §6(d) is common practice, but the distributor keeps responsibility for availability, so it carries residual risk. (3) Your commercial EULA must not restrict customers' AGPL rights in those components (§10). Clauses such as "no reverse engineering / no modification / no redistribution" need an explicit carve-out for open-source components. (4) No §13 duty, because the code is unmodified.
- **"Unmodified" should be read narrowly.** Configuration files, provisioning YAML, dashboard JSON and Helm values are generally data or configuration rather than modifications (interpretation). Things that likely count as modifications: patching source, rebuilding with code changes, rebranding or white-labeling the Grafana UI, removing logos, and back-porting security fixes into your own fork. White-labeling also raises trademark questions, and Grafana's OEM program sells "custom branding" for exactly that case.
- **Modified AGPL components.** Distribution duties apply, and the modifications are AGPL. §13 also requires the modified version itself to "prominently offer" its source to every user interacting with it over the network, which means the customer's operators using the vendor's UI. The vendor has to build that offer (for example, a "Source" link) into the modified UI or service.
- **Embedding/linking.** Embedding Grafana panels through an iframe or calling Grafana/Loki/Tempo HTTP APIs from the vendor's UI fits the FSF "separate programs communicating over sockets" model, so the vendor's own code stays outside the AGPL (FSF interpretation). Copying or compiling Grafana's **AGPL** frontend or backend code into the vendor's application would likely create a combined work under AGPL. Grafana's Apache-2.0 packages (`@grafana/ui`, `grafana-data`, `grafana-runtime`, scenes, plugin SDK) can be reused under Apache-2.0.
- **Vendor-written Grafana plugins** run inside the Grafana process or the browser bundle. Grafana says "plugins … remain Apache-licensed" and its SDKs are Apache-2.0, which supports keeping proprietary plugins proprietary. But Grafana's FAQ does not discuss the derivative-work question for in-process plugins. Get legal review if you plan proprietary plugins.
- **Commercial and procurement risk beyond legal compliance.** Security and defense buyers often run license scanners and have AGPL policies like Google's. Shipping AGPL inside the product can block deals even when the vendor is fully compliant. That is a reasonable basis for the "HIGH risk" rating, though the risk is commercial and operational more than a legal "your code becomes AGPL" risk.
- **Effective options for the Grafana stack.** (a) Ship unmodified AGPL OSS and run a source-distribution and compliance process. (b) Negotiate an OEM/commercial license with Grafana Labs (the free Enterprise binary is not redistributable). (c) Replace the stack with permissive components (Q2). Option (a) also commits the vendor to never patching Grafana/Loki/Tempo/Mimir locally without triggering §13.

### Gaps
- No public terms, pricing or scope for the Grafana OEM license, including whether it covers Loki, Mimir or Tempo (GEL/GEM/GET) for on-prem redistribution. Ask oem@grafana.com.
- No court decision was found on whether the AGPL boundary holds for separate processes over HTTP. The FSF FAQ is interpretive.
- Within budget I did not find an authoritative law-firm or OSPO article specifically on AGPL in vendor on-prem bundles. Search results were low-quality blogs, so I did not cite them.
- The 2021 Grafana FAQ promised Enterprise-style binaries for Loki and Tempo ("We expect to offer similar versions…"). I did not verify whether those exist today or are redistributable. The Grafana Labs License text does cover "Grafana Enterprise Metrics" and "Grafana Enterprise Logs".

## Q2. Which permissive (Apache-2.0 / MIT) alternatives exist as of 2026, what are their exact licenses (including open-core splits), and what license changes happened in 2023–2026?

### Takeaway
A fully permissive stack is feasible in 2026. Metrics: Prometheus, Thanos, Cortex and VictoriaMetrics are all Apache-2.0. Logs: VictoriaLogs, OpenSearch, ClickHouse and Quickwit (Apache-2.0 again since January 2025). Traces: Jaeger v2, VictoriaTraces, OpenSearch Data Prepper, ClickHouse-based stacks. UI: Perses, Jaeger UI, vmui, OpenSearch Dashboards, HyperDX/ClickStack (MIT). Watch the open-core edges: VictoriaMetrics enterprise features (downsampling, multi-retention, mTLS, backup manager) need a proprietary license key, and SigNoz's `ee/` directory is under a restrictive enterprise license. Avoid OpenObserve (AGPL since November 2023), Uptrace (BSL, then AGPL since October 2023) and Elastic (SSPL/ELv2/AGPL tri-license) if the goal is "no AGPL".

### Inferences
- **License-clean candidate stacks, with no AGPL in the shipped runtime:**
  - **A. "Victoria" stack.** VictoriaMetrics (or Prometheus + VictoriaMetrics), VictoriaLogs, and VictoriaTraces or Jaeger v2. UI from vmui, the VictoriaLogs UI, Jaeger UI and Perses. All Apache-2.0, no object store required. Two risks: one company (VictoriaMetrics Inc.) controls all three components, and VictoriaTraces is still v0.x.
  - **B. CNCF-only stack.** Prometheus + Thanos (Thanos needs an S3-compatible store), Jaeger v2 on OpenSearch, OpenSearch for logs, Perses or OpenSearch Dashboards for UI. All Apache-2.0 and foundation-governed (CNCF / Linux Foundation OpenSearch Foundation). Heavier, especially the JVM-based OpenSearch.
  - **C. ClickHouse stack.** ClickStack/HyperDX (MIT) + ClickHouse (Apache-2.0) + OTel Collector. Or SigNoz community (MIT), as long as the build does not include `ee/` or `cmd/enterprise/` code. Verify which code ends up in the shipped image.
- **Open-core traps to design around:**
  - VictoriaMetrics downsampling and multi-retention are enterprise-only, so long-retention designs would need a commercial license.
  - The SigNoz enterprise directory forbids redistribution.
  - The VictoriaMetrics Grafana datasource plugin is AGPL. Use the stock Prometheus datasource or another UI if Grafana is used at all.
- **Relicensing is a real pattern:** Grafana 2021, Elastic 2021/2024, OpenObserve and Uptrace 2023, MinIO's 2025–2026 withdrawal, and the reverse move by Quickwit in 2025. Prefer foundation-governed projects (CNCF: Prometheus, Thanos, Cortex, Jaeger, Perses; Apache: SkyWalking; LF: OpenSearch) or plan to pin and fork if a single-company project changes license. Relicensing normally applies to new releases; already-released Apache versions keep their license (general principle, not a project-specific finding).
- If Mimir, Loki, Tempo or Thanos are kept, the S3-compatible store is a second licensing and maintenance decision. MinIO, the default choice, is now archived and AGPL.

### Gaps
- I did not enumerate which SigNoz features sit in `ee/` (SSO, RBAC and so on), or whether official SigNoz Docker images include `ee/` code. Inspect the images before shipping.
- I did not fetch the ELv2 and SSPL terms themselves, so the exact restrictions on bundling Elastic binaries in a commercial on-prem product are not documented here.
- Quickwit's long-term roadmap under Datadog is uncertain. Releases continue, but no public commitment beyond the January 2025 blog was found.
- CNCF maturity levels for Thanos and Cortex were not re-verified this session.

## Q3. What published benchmarks or vendor-neutral measurements exist for footprint (RAM/CPU/disk) at ~20–100 services, and how suitable is each option for air-gapped packaging?

### Takeaway
Truly vendor-neutral benchmarks are scarce. Most comparisons come from VictoriaMetrics, SigNoz or ClickHouse, or from third parties with a stake. The consistent signal across vendor and independent sources: VictoriaMetrics and VictoriaLogs run with markedly less RAM and disk than Prometheus/Mimir and Loki/Elasticsearch, and ClickHouse compresses logs very well. Grafana's own sizing docs show Mimir and Loki in microservices mode are designed for far larger scale than 20–100 services. For air-gap, single-binary, no-object-store systems (VictoriaMetrics, VictoriaLogs, VictoriaTraces, Prometheus, Jaeger with Badger, ClickHouse) are simplest. Mimir, Loki, Tempo and Thanos need an S3-compatible store on-prem. Grafana has phone-home settings that must be turned off.

### Cited Findings

**Metrics**
- Prometheus (project docs): "Prometheus stores an average of only 1-2 bytes per sample." Sizing formula: "needed_disk_space = retention_time_seconds * ingested_samples_per_second * bytes_per_sample". Also: "local storage … is not clustered or replicated … should be managed like any other single node database." — [Prometheus storage docs](https://prometheus.io/docs/prometheus/latest/storage/)
- VictoriaMetrics (vendor docs, based on its case studies): a single node "works perfectly" at "Ingestion rate: 1.5+ million samples per second; Active time series: 50+ million; … Queries: 200+ qps". It recommends keeping "50% of free RAM", "50% of spare CPU" and "At least 20% of free storage space". The docs claim "up to 7x less RAM than Prometheus, Thanos or Cortex". — [VictoriaMetrics single-node docs, Capacity planning](https://docs.victoriametrics.com/victoriametrics/single-server-victoriametrics/) **(vendor claim)**
- **VictoriaMetrics vs Mimir benchmark (vendor-authored by VictoriaMetrics, September 2022).** Setup: Mimir 2.2.0 vs VictoriaMetrics 1.80.0-cluster on GKE e2-standard-16 nodes, ~5.5M active series, 360k samples/s, 24h, RF 3 (Mimir) vs RF 2 (VictoriaMetrics). Results: average CPU ~30 cores (Mimir) vs ~18 cores (VictoriaMetrics); memory "x5 less RAM" for VictoriaMetrics (Mimir ingesters at 80–90% of ~283GB allocated); disk after 24h 369GB local + 149.7GB object store (Mimir) vs 49GB (VictoriaMetrics). Caveats stated in the post: Mimir compaction was incomplete over 24h, replication factors differed, and only instant queries were tested. — [VictoriaMetrics blog: Grafana Mimir and VictoriaMetrics performance tests](https://victoriametrics.com/blog/mimir-benchmark/) **(vendor-authored)**
- **Independent but small-scale (dbi services consultancy, June 2023):** with an Avalanche load generator, Prometheus memory kept growing until it was "killed by Out-Of-Memory", while VictoriaMetrics used "at most 1GB memory". Storage after 2h was 1.8 GB (Prometheus) vs 1.2 GB (VictoriaMetrics). Versions were not stated. — [dbi services blog](https://www.dbi-services.com/blog/prometheus-vs-victoriametrics/) (independent; weak methodology)
- **Mimir sizing (Grafana docs).** Distributor "1 core every 25,000 samples per second" and "1GB every 25,000 samples per second". Ingester "1 core for every 300,000 series in memory", "2.5GB for every 300,000 series in memory", "5GB [disk] for every 300,000 series in memory", with replication factor 3 by default. Store-gateway disk "13GB every 1 million active series". Compactor: 1 core, 4GB, 300GB disk per instance for each 20M series. Querier "1 core for every 10 queries per second" and "1GB for every 10 queries per second". — [Grafana Mimir: Planning capacity](https://grafana.com/docs/mimir/latest/manage/run-production-environment/planning-capacity/) **(vendor sizing guidance)**

**Logs**
- **Loki sizing (Grafana docs).** The smallest published tier is "Less than 100TB/month (3TB/day)": 38 CPU cores and 59 Gi memory in total, with 6 ingesters, 4 distributors and 10 queriers. Microservices mode, p90 utilization. — [Grafana Loki: Size the cluster](https://grafana.com/docs/loki/latest/setup/size/) **(vendor sizing guidance)**
- **VictoriaLogs (vendor docs):** "uses up to 30x less RAM and up to 15x less disk space than other solutions such as Elasticsearch and Grafana Loki." Cites a user who "replaced 27-node Elasticsearch with a single-node VictoriaLogs". — [VictoriaLogs docs](https://docs.victoriametrics.com/victorialogs/) **(vendor claim)**
- **Loki vs VictoriaLogs (TrueFoundry, June 2026).** Third party: TrueFoundry develops neither product, but it is a company blog. Setup: 4 vCPU / 8 GiB each, single-binary for both, ~500 GB over 7 days, flog at 65 MB/s via Vector, block cache disabled. Results: peak ingestion 20 MB/s (Loki) vs 66 MB/s (VictoriaLogs); peak CPU 4 vCPU throttled vs 1.1 vCPU; steady memory 6–7 GiB vs 0.6–2 GiB; 7-day storage 501 GiB vs 318 GiB; needle-in-haystack query 12 s vs ~0.9 s. — [TrueFoundry: VictoriaLogs vs Loki](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/victorialogs-vs-loki) (third-party; defaults-only config)
- **SigNoz logs benchmark (vendor-authored by SigNoz, ~2022).** Single c6a.4xlarge (16 vCPU / 32 GB), 500 GB of logs each, versions ES 8.4.3, Loki 2.6.1, ClickHouse 22.4.5. Ingestion ~55K logs/s (SigNoz/ClickHouse) vs ~20K (Elasticsearch) vs ~21K (Loki). Memory ~6 GB vs ~18 GB vs ~9.6 GB. Storage 207.9 GB vs 388 GB vs 142.2 GB (Loki with limited indexing). Loki could not return results for high-cardinality queries. — [SigNoz logs performance benchmark](https://signoz.io/blog/logs-performance-benchmark/), [repo](https://github.com/SigNoz/logs-benchmark) **(vendor-authored; old versions)**
- **ClickHouse log compression (vendor-authored by ClickHouse, October 2025).** 66.75M nginx log lines, 20 GB raw. Plain ZSTD(3) file gives 38x; structured default 35x; with types/codecs 92x; with an optimized ordering key 178x (109 MB). Time-ordered layout gives 53x. — [ClickHouse blog: Compressing nginx logs 170x](https://clickhouse.com/blog/log-compression-170x) **(vendor-authored; best-case dataset)**
- **OpenSearch sizing (AWS guidance for OpenSearch Service, generally applicable).** "Source data * (1 + number of replicas) * 1.45 = minimum storage requirement" — [AWS: Calculating storage requirements](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/opensearch-service/latest/developerguide/bp-storage.html). Shard guidance: 30–50 GiB shards for log workloads and ≤25 shards per GiB of JVM heap — [AWS: Choosing the number of shards](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/opensearch-service/latest/developerguide/bp-sharding.html)

**Traces**
- **Tempo sizing (Grafana docs).** Distributor "1 replica per every 10MB/s of received traffic", 2 cores, 2 GB. Live-store "1 replica per every 6-10MB/s", 1 core, "Mem: 4-20GB". Block-builder 0.5 core, 5–10GB. Querier "1 replica per every 1-2MB/s of received traffic", 4–20GB. Query-frontend 2 replicas. Backend-scheduler and worker 0.5 core, 1–2GB each. — [Grafana Tempo: Size the cluster](https://grafana.com/docs/tempo/latest/set-up-for-tracing/setup-tempo/plan/size/) **(vendor sizing guidance)**
- **VictoriaTraces (vendor docs):** "uses up to 3.7x less RAM and up to 2.6x less CPU than other solutions such as Grafana Tempo" and "has no additional storage dependencies (such as object storage or external databases like ClickHouse and Elasticsearch)". — [VictoriaTraces docs](https://docs.victoriametrics.com/victoriatraces/) **(vendor claim)**
- **Jaeger (project docs):** recommends OpenSearch over Cassandra at large scale and "short TTL on trace data (e.g. two weeks) to save storage costs". — [Jaeger storage docs](https://www.jaegertracing.io/docs/latest/storage/)
- Tempo vs Jaeger cost: Tempo is commonly described as cheaper because it stores unindexed traces in object storage instead of Elasticsearch/Cassandra — [Last9 blog](https://last9.io/blog/grafana-tempo-vs-jaeger/) (vendor-adjacent blog; no measured numbers).

**All-in-one stacks**
- SigNoz resource-planning table, with no throughput mapping: collectors 3 × (4 cores, 16 GiB); ClickHouse 2 shards × (16 cores, 32 GiB); SigNoz core 4 cores / 8 GiB; PostgreSQL 2 cores / 8 GiB; ZooKeeper 3 × (2 cores, 8 GiB). — [SigNoz resources planning](https://signoz.io/docs/setup/capacity-planning/community/resources-planning) **(vendor)**

**Air-gap packaging**
- VictoriaMetrics "consists of a single small executable without external dependencies". Binaries, Docker Hub/Quay images and Helm charts are available. — [VictoriaMetrics docs](https://docs.victoriametrics.com/victoriametrics/single-server-victoriametrics/). VictoriaTraces offers "helm charts and the Operator" — [VictoriaTraces docs](https://docs.victoriametrics.com/victoriatraces/). Enterprise license keys can be verified offline with `-license.forceOffline` — [Enterprise docs](https://docs.victoriametrics.com/victoriametrics/enterprise/)
- Jaeger v2 "is released as a single binary or container image" with roles such as all-in-one, collector, query and ingester. — [Jaeger deployment docs](https://www.jaegertracing.io/docs/latest/deployment/)
- Grafana phone-home settings to disable for air-gapped installs. `reporting_enabled`: Grafana "sends anonymous usage statistics to stats.grafana.org … every 24 hours. Default value is true". `check_for_updates` checks GitHub "every ten minutes". `check_for_plugin_updates` checks grafana.com. — [Grafana configuration docs](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/setup-grafana/configure-grafana/)
- SUSE Rancher's monitoring docs describe dashboard ConfigMaps as a method "suitable for air-gapped installations". — [Rancher Monitoring and Alerting](https://ranchermanager.docs.rancher.com/integrations-in-rancher/monitoring-and-alerting)

### Inferences
**Illustrative sizing for 20–100 services.** These figures apply the cited formulas to *assumed* workloads; they are not measurements.
- **Metrics.** Assume 100 services × ~5k series each ≈ 500k active series at a 30 s scrape, which is ≈16.7k samples/s.
  - Prometheus disk at 1–2 B/sample over 15 days: 16.7k × 1.296M s × 1–2 B ≈ 22–43 GB.
  - Mimir at RF 3 holds 1.5M series in ingesters: by Grafana's ratios ≈ 5 cores, ~12.5 GB RAM and ~25 GB ingester disk. Add distributors (≈0.7 core / 0.7 GB), queriers, store-gateways, compactor and object storage. That is 7+ component types for a workload a single Prometheus or VictoriaMetrics node handles.
- **Logs.** At 20–100 services, daily volume is plausibly tens to low hundreds of GB/day (an assumption). That is far below Loki's smallest published tier (<3 TB/day, 38 cores / 59 Gi). Grafana publishes no small-tier numbers for monolithic or simple-scalable mode (gap).
  - OpenSearch at an assumed 50 GB/day × 14 days × 1 replica ≈ 700 GB × 2 × 1.45 ≈ 2 TB of disk, plus JVM heap planning.
  - VictoriaLogs and ClickHouse would likely need several times less disk, per the vendor and third-party data above.
- **Traces.** At this scale (likely ≤10 MB/s of spans, an assumption), Tempo's minimum HA layout is still about 6 component types plus object storage. Jaeger v2 with OpenSearch, or VictoriaTraces single-node, is simpler. VictoriaTraces is still v0.x.
- **Air-gap suitability ranking** (judgment):
  1. Most suitable: VictoriaMetrics, VictoriaLogs, VictoriaTraces, Prometheus, Jaeger with Badger/OpenSearch. Single static binaries, local disk, Apache Helm charts.
  2. Medium: ClickHouse/ClickStack and SigNoz. Several stateful components (ClickHouse, Keeper/ZooKeeper, and Postgres for SigNoz) but no object store required. OpenSearch is JVM-heavy and needs shard/heap tuning.
  3. Least suitable: Mimir, Loki, Tempo and Thanos in distributed mode. They need an S3-compatible store on-prem, and the de-facto default, MinIO, is archived/AGPL. Loki and Tempo can run monolithically on local filesystems, but that loses HA.
- The two-tier OTel Collector design keeps backend choice swappable through OTLP and Prometheus remote-write. VictoriaMetrics, VictoriaLogs and VictoriaTraces, Jaeger v2, ClickStack and SigNoz all ingest OTLP (per their docs above). This lowers lock-in whichever backend is chosen.

### Gaps
- **No truly vendor-neutral, reproducible benchmark** was found that compares Prometheus, VictoriaMetrics and Mimir, or Loki, VictoriaLogs, OpenSearch and ClickHouse, on the same recent versions. All the large-scale numbers above are vendor-authored or single-company blogs.
- I could not retrieve the frequently cited Valialkin (VictoriaMetrics CTO) Prometheus vs VictoriaMetrics node_exporter benchmark from Medium, so its figures (e.g., "4.3GB vs 23GB RAM") are not included as verified.
- No published small-footprint (monolithic/SSD) sizing from Grafana for Loki, Tempo or Mimir at the scale of 20–100 services.
- No footprint data found for Apache SkyWalking, Quickwit or OpenObserve within budget.
- No published per-spans/s benchmark for Tempo vs Jaeger storage costs with real numbers.

## Q4. What is the dashboard/UI story without Grafana, and can Grafana OSS (AGPL) run as an unmodified separate process without obligations on the vendor's own code?

### Takeaway
Credible non-AGPL UIs exist, but none matches Grafana's breadth:
- **Perses** (Apache-2.0, CNCF Sandbox, pre-1.0) is the strategic Grafana replacement for dashboards. Red Hat now ships a "Red Hat build of Perses" (tech preview, April 2026) with Grafana-dashboard import.
- Per-signal built-in UIs: **vmui** and the VictoriaLogs web UI, **Jaeger UI**, **OpenSearch Dashboards**.
- All-in-one UIs: **HyperDX/ClickStack** (MIT) and **SigNoz** (MIT core).

Grafana OSS can be shipped as an unmodified separate container without pulling the vendor's own code under AGPL (license text plus FSF interpretation). The vendor still has to meet the AGPL distribution obligations for Grafana itself (Q1).

### Cited Findings
- Perses: Apache-2.0, CNCF Sandbox since August 29, 2024, latest stable v0.54.0 (July 2026) — [CNCF Perses](https://www.cncf.io/projects/perses/), [perses releases](https://github.com/perses/perses/releases). Red Hat introduced a "technology preview of the Red Hat build of Perses in the cluster observability operator 1.4" on April 2, 2026. It has an import feature to "upload or paste Grafana JSON or YAML configurations, automatically detected and converted into PersesDashboard resources", is integrated as an OpenShift console plugin, and has native Kubernetes RBAC filtering. — [Red Hat Developer: Red Hat build of Perses](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2026/04/02/red-hat-build-perses-cluster-observability-operator)
- vmui: "VictoriaMetrics provides UI for query troubleshooting and exploration", available at `/vmui`. It includes a query tab, top queries, a metrics explorer and a cardinality explorer. — [VictoriaMetrics docs: vmui](https://docs.victoriametrics.com/victoriametrics/single-server-victoriametrics/)
- VictoriaLogs "provides built-in web UI for logs' exploration" and a Grafana plugin (Apache-2.0). — [VictoriaLogs docs](https://docs.victoriametrics.com/victorialogs/), [victorialogs-datasource](https://github.com/VictoriaMetrics/victorialogs-datasource)
- VictoriaTraces exposes Jaeger Query APIs "to integrate with Grafana or Jaeger Frontend". — [VictoriaTraces docs](https://docs.victoriametrics.com/victoriatraces/)
- Jaeger UI ships with Jaeger (Apache-2.0); the docs have a "Frontend/UI Configuration" section. — [Jaeger deployment docs](https://www.jaegertracing.io/docs/latest/deployment/)
- OpenSearch Dashboards is Apache-2.0. — [OpenSearch-Dashboards LICENSE](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch-Dashboards/blob/main/LICENSE.txt)
- ClickStack UI (HyperDX) is "a purpose-built frontend for exploring and visualizing observability data" — [ClickStack overview](https://clickhouse.com/docs/clickstack/overview); its license is MIT — [hyperdx LICENSE](https://github.com/hyperdxio/hyperdx/blob/main/LICENSE). The SigNoz UI is MIT outside `ee/` — [SigNoz LICENSE](https://github.com/SigNoz/signoz/blob/main/LICENSE)
- Grafana's reusable frontend packages `@grafana/ui`, `grafana-data` and `grafana-runtime` are Apache-2.0 — [grafana LICENSING.md](https://github.com/grafana/grafana/blob/main/LICENSING.md)
- Grafana's position on unmodified distribution: "Unmodified distributions are not affected" — [Grafana CEO Q&A](https://grafana.com/blog/2021/04/20/qa-with-our-ceo-on-relicensing/). The license text confirms that §13 triggers only "if you modify the Program" and that inclusion in an aggregate "does not cause this License to apply to the other parts" — [AGPLv3 text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.txt)

### Inferences
- **Practical UI options for the Helm kit:**
  - **Permissive, lighter.** Perses for curated dashboards plus native UIs (vmui, VictoriaLogs UI, Jaeger UI) for exploration. Or ClickStack/HyperDX as a single correlated UI if the ClickHouse path is chosen.
  - **Grafana OSS unmodified as an optional add-on.** Keep it as a separate image and chart. The vendor's own UI should link to it or iframe it, never compile in its AGPL code. Run the AGPL compliance process (license, notices, source on media), disable phone-home, and never patch it.
- Perses' Grafana-dashboard import and Red Hat's adoption suggest a credible migration path. Perses is still v0.x and Sandbox-level, though, so expect UI gaps (fewer panel types and plugins than Grafana) and API churn.
- The vendor's existing product UI can deep-link into Jaeger UI, vmui or Perses dashboards with no licensing impact (all Apache-2.0).

### Gaps
- No feature-parity comparison (panel types, alerting UI, correlations) between Perses and Grafana was researched.
- I did not verify the current state of OpenSearch Dashboards' Trace Analytics / Observability plugin for OTel traces.
- Whether a vendor-written proprietary Grafana plugin is a derivative of AGPL Grafana is not addressed by Grafana's FAQ (see Q1).

## Q5. How do other commercial vendors that ship on-prem / air-gapped products handle this (GitLab, Rancher, OpenShift, Elastic, others)?

### Takeaway
Practice splits. **GitLab** (which ships a self-managed product) froze Grafana at the last Apache release (7.5) "to allow time to consider the implications of the newer AGPL-licensed releases", then deprecated (16.0) and removed (16.3) bundled Grafana. **Red Hat** removed Grafana from the OpenShift monitoring stack (keeping Apache-licensed Prometheus, Thanos and Alertmanager plus its own console), moved toward Perses, but *does* ship AGPL Loki and Tempo as supported operators. As an all-open-source vendor, Red Hat already publishes all source, which Grafana's CEO explicitly called "no issue". **SUSE Rancher** ships Grafana inside `rancher-monitoring`. **Grafana OEM partners** (Siemens, Thales, Canon and others) buy a commercial license. **Google** bans AGPL outright.

### Cited Findings
- **GitLab (self-managed Omnibus):** "In GitLab 14.4 the provided Grafana version is 7.5, this is a downgrade from the Grafana 8.1 version introduced in GitLab 14.3. This was reverted to an Apache-licensed Grafana release to allow time to consider the implications of the newer AGPL-licensed releases." — [GitLab 14 specific changes (Omnibus)](https://archives.docs.gitlab.com/16.2/omnibus/update/gitlab_14_changes.html)
- GitLab: "Bundled Grafana was deprecated GitLab 16.0 and is no longer supported. It will be removed in GitLab 16.3." — [GitLab docs: Grafana Dashboard Service (deprecated)](https://archives.docs.gitlab.com/16.2/omnibus/settings/grafana.html). The deprecation issue cites the discontinuation of the "Grafana Observability UI" GitLab had planned to swap to. — [omnibus-gitlab issue #7772](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/omnibus-gitlab/-/issues/7772)
- **Red Hat OpenShift monitoring (4.11):** the Cluster Monitoring Operator manages "Prometheus and Alertmanager instances, Thanos Querier, Telemeter Client". — [OpenShift 4.11 monitoring overview](https://docs.redhat.com/fr/documentation/openshift_container_platform/4.11/html/monitoring/monitoring-overview). The Grafana UI was deprecated and removed in 4.10/4.11 per a Red Hat KB title and snippet — [Red Hat KB 4605641](https://access.redhat.com/solutions/4605641) (403 when fetched; snippet only). This **conflicts** with "deprecated in OpenShift 4.11 and removed in OpenShift 4.12" — [Red Hat Cloud Experts: Deploying Grafana on OpenShift 4](https://cloud.redhat.com/experts/o11y/ocp-grafana/)
- Red Hat ships Grafana Tempo through the Tempo Operator in "Red Hat OpenShift distributed tracing platform" (3.x) and Loki through the Loki Operator (LokiStack) for OpenShift Logging. — [Red Hat distributed tracing docs](https://docs.redhat.com/en/documentation/red_hat_openshift_distributed_tracing_platform/), [Red Hat Developer: Tempo operator](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2023/08/01/how-deploy-new-grafana-tempo-operator-openshift)
- Red Hat build of Perses (tech preview, COO 1.4, April 2026) for OpenShift console dashboards. — [Red Hat Developer](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2026/04/02/red-hat-build-perses-cluster-observability-operator)
- Grafana's CEO on OpenShift and Cloud Foundry: "that source is already open, so there is no issue." — [Grafana CEO Q&A](https://grafana.com/blog/2021/04/20/qa-with-our-ceo-on-relicensing/)
- **SUSE Rancher:** `rancher-monitoring` is "powered by Prometheus, Grafana, Alertmanager, the Prometheus Operator, and the Prometheus adapter". For K3s it advises a Prometheus memory limit of 2500 Mi and a request of 1750 Mi. — [Rancher Monitoring and Alerting](https://ranchermanager.docs.rancher.com/integrations-in-rancher/monitoring-and-alerting)
- **Grafana OEM partners:** Siemens, Google Cloud, Thales, Canon, Comact, Sitech. — [Grafana OEM partners](https://www.grafana.com/partnerships/oem-partners/)
- **Elastic:** itself moved Apache → SSPL/ELv2 (2021) and added AGPL as an option (August 2024). — [Elastic blog](https://www.elastic.co/blog/elasticsearch-is-open-source-again)
- **Google:** "Code licensed under the GNU Affero General Public License (AGPL) MUST NOT be used at Google." — [Google AGPL policy](https://opensource.google/documentation/reference/using/agpl-policy)

### Inferences
- The GitLab case is the closest analogue to the vendor's situation: a commercial, self-managed product bundling observability for customers, including air-gapped ones. GitLab's documented response was to avoid shipping AGPL Grafana, pinning to Apache 7.5 until it went out of support, and then to drop bundled Grafana entirely. The issue states a product reason, but the 14.4 note shows licensing drove the pin. A pinned pre-AGPL Grafana 7.5 is **not** a viable option in 2026, since it is long out of security support (inference; GitLab notes it is "no longer supported").
- Red Hat and SUSE can ship AGPL components comfortably because their whole business model publishes source. That reasoning does not carry over to a proprietary security vendor whose own product is closed. Their architecture choices still help, though: Red Hat moved the default UI to its own console plus Perses and kept AGPL components as separately installed operators.
- A pattern consistent with these vendors: keep the default bundled stack permissive (Prometheus/Thanos/Alertmanager-style), and offer AGPL components as optional, separately installed, unmodified add-ons, or cover them with a commercial license.

### Gaps
- Not verified this session: whether GitLab Omnibus still bundles Prometheus and exporters in 2026, and under which license (Prometheus is Apache-2.0).
- Elastic's own on-prem ECK/air-gap distribution terms under ELv2 were not reviewed.
- The exact OpenShift version that removed Grafana is in conflict between Red Hat sources (4.11 vs 4.12).
- No public statements found from other security vendors (e.g., Palo Alto, Fortinet, CrowdStrike on-prem appliances) about how they handle bundled observability backends.
